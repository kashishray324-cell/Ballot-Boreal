# Product proposal

## Considered concepts

| Concept | Pitch | Private | Public | Gemini role | Visual direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ballot Boreal** | An association member casts one eligibility-proven, anonymous civic ballot. | Credential, identity, vote selection, proving material. | Ballot rules, valid proof outcome, nullifier, aggregate. | Explains public ballot rules and disclosure choices. | Nordic civic minimalism. |
| **Quiet Quorum** | A private threshold poll lets a board prove quorum without exposing attendance. | Member roster and attendance. | Threshold reached/not reached, aggregate count. | Converts a public meeting rule into plain language. | Swiss information design. |
| **Harbor Key** | A venue gate verifies age/eligibility without collecting a document scan. | Date of birth, credential and identifier. | Valid/invalid policy outcome and scope. | Identifies ambiguity in public venue policy wording. | Contemporary museum interface. |

## Selected: Ballot Boreal

Ballot Boreal won because privacy is intrinsic: a vote system must ensure one eligible vote without publishing an identity-to-vote trail. Its selective disclosure is concrete, explainable in one minute, and feasible for an MVP. The anonymous nullifier makes the anti-replay story visible, while the voter journey makes a normally invisible privacy boundary understandable.

**Target users:** cooperatives, student unions, membership associations, and their voters.

**Real-world problem:** centralised voting products retain identity, eligibility, and participation data even when the organization only needs eligibility and a valid one-time ballot.

**Why Midnight:** the contract evaluates a private witness against public issuer and ballot requirements, with only minimal public state and deliberate disclosures.
